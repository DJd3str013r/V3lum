# Contract additions - WP23 (wave 4)

Correlation packs P171 (threat-intel consensus, CR27-CR30), P291 (attack surface, CR07, CR08,
CR13-CR16, CR25, CR26, CR31) and P027 (e-mail spoofing, CR01-CR03). Nothing of the frozen guide
changes: the rules use the section 16 `@rule` API (`fn(graph, results, rctx)`), ship positive and
negative graph fixtures under `tests/fixtures/rules/<rule_id>/` and declare their vocabulary and
ATT&CK ids in pack fragments. This file lists what WP23 adds and the conventions **module authors**
follow so their facts reach these rules.

Who needs it: authors of **threat-intel feed modules** (CR27 votes), **scanner/indexed-scan modules**
(ports, CVEs, JARM, favicons), **DNS/takeover modules** (dangling CNAMEs), the **e-mail posture
producers** (CR01-CR03 inputs), **WP22** (rule listing, precision demotion over these rules),
**WP19** (risk model: downgraded/`context_only` correlations), **WP14/WP20** (renderers: CR03 is INFO
in the terminal) and **WP29** (notices: `can_i_take_over_xyz`).

## Details

### CR27: how a module's verdict becomes a vote

A finding is a vote when it carries a tag in `VOTE_TAGS` (`malicious`, `phishing`, `malware`, `c2`,
`botnet`) and passes `evidence_ok` (suppressed/`context_only` findings never vote). Its family comes
from the provider registry:

* `Finding.source` = a registry source id -> `upstream_family`. A source with `embeds` (an aggregate:
  FireHOL, IPsum, OTX...) is an *ambiguous* vote: it counts only when none of its embedded families
  voted directly and it overlaps no other counted aggregate.
* `data['upstream_families']` (a list) names the families that actually answered, within the
  source's declared families (`upstream_family` + `embeds`): OTX pulses relaying abuse.ch write
  `['abuse.ch']`, independent community pulses `['otx']`. Undeclared families are ignored.
* `data['hits']` = `[{source, listed_at | last_seen | last_online, currency}]` (aggregated blocklist
  findings): one vote per hit, each with its own date; `currency: 'historical'` marks history.
* Without `source`, the module's declared `sources` are used (one source: that source; several: one
  ambiguous vote). A module without registry sources is 'unattributed': such verdicts count only when
  two modules agree, and all of them together form one family.
* A `malicious: true` attribute from a module that produced no voting finding is one vote of that
  module's sources. On sinkholes and warninglisted values no listing votes (engine parity).
* Dates: `observed_last` (else `observed_first`) is the listing's last_seen; outside the decay window
  (ip/url/cidr 30 days, domain 90 days, IaaS 'cloud' addresses 14 days) it is history. Undated votes
  count unless tagged `historical`.
* Not counted: sources reported `licence` in any result's `source_statuses` or listed in
  `rctx.meta['licence_blocked_sources']`, registry sources with terms class 'unknown', and domain
  votes whose finding data (`ip`, `address`, `resolved_ip`, `via_ip`, `listed_ip`) names a shared
  address the domain resolves to.

Severity: HIGH with >= 2 families (confirmed with >= 3), MEDIUM with 1. Benign scanners (noise kind
or tag `benign_scanner`, attribute `scanner_classification: 'benign'`), allow-listed values
(`known_good` findings, attribute `allowlisted`) and shared CDN/SaaS/hosting addresses keep the
correlation, capped at LOW and downgraded to POSSIBLE through `Correlation.downgrade` with the rule
`SUP-benign_scanner`, `SUP-allowlist` or `SUP-shared_address` (never deleted). Two more downgrades
(review): other noise ranges (`node.noise` with a kind outside `NOISE_KEEP_KINDS`, e.g. resolvers,
webmail, private: `SUP-noise`) and anonymity infrastructure (`is_tor_exit`/`is_vpn`/`is_proxy`
true, or a `tor`/`vpn`/`proxy`/`anonymizer` tag: `SUP-anonymizer`, P171 cond 7) - capped at LOW,
POSSIBLE, never tagged `malicious`; the anonymizer case is also tagged `context_only`. A downgraded
consensus says so in its title (`(downgraded: benign_scanner, ...)`). An analyst false-positive
verdict takes a listing out of the count, and the `malicious` attribute of a module whose listing
is no vote never votes in its place. Suppressed (`SUP-*`/`context_only`) listings and listings on
sinkholed or warninglisted entities are no votes (the engine-stage semantics that
`tests/test_engine_suppression.py` fixes); they stay listed in the evidence. The evidence lists
every counted source with its family, last_seen and terms class, the deduplicated sources, the
history and the excluded listings; it also names the finding ids. **Behaviour change** (documented
in `tests/test_engine.py::test_multi_source_malicious_and_risk`): the legacy rule gave CRITICAL as
soon as two module names flagged an entity; the pack rule gives HIGH for two families and MEDIUM for
one. `policy.CONSENSUS_RULES` (hostile-host guard) keeps matching the id.

### Attack surface: facts the rules read

* Ports (CR13/CR14/CR25): `open_ports` (ints), `services` (`'3389/tcp ...'` strings or `{port}`
  dicts) and `port_service` neighbours (`host:port/proto`). The observation date is the newest
  `observed_last`/`observed_first` of the reporting module's evidence findings or its `last_seen`
  attribute; undated or older than 30 days lowers the confidence one level. A report from a module
  with `opsec = ACTIVE` (an authorized check) starts at CONFIRMED.
* CVEs (CR15/CR16/alias): `has_vuln` edges to CVE nodes, `vulns` (ids), `vulns_verified` (ids the
  source verified), finding data `cve`/`cves` and `vulns` items (`{cve, cvss, verified |
  verified_by_shodan}`, as shodan_api writes them); KEV/EPSS/CVSS from the CVE node (`kev`, `epss`,
  `cvss`, `cvss_scores`), else the item's `cvss` and the local `cisa_kev`/`epss_scores` copies (read
  once per graph through `prime_local_scores`). *Verified* = in `vulns_verified`, a `vulns` item
  flagged `verified`/`verified_by_shodan: true` (even inside a `version_inferred` finding), or
  an evidence finding tagged `vulnerable`/`kev`/`exploit_available` that names the CVE and is not
  tagged `version_inferred` (an ACTIVE module's finding reads 'authorized ACTIVE check'). Products for
  CR16 come from the host `cpes` matched with the CVE node's `affected`/`cpe_matches`/`cpes`.
* Takeover (CR07): `cname_to` edges; dangling evidence = the target's `valid: false` (dns_records,
  NXDOMAIN confirmed by a second resolver), `cname_dangling` with `confirmed: true`, or a
  `takeover_fingerprint` whose `service` names a content-fingerprint entry. Only entries marked
  vulnerable in `takeover_fingerprints.json` count.
* Dangling cloud address (CR08): an A record to a cloud address (noise kind `cloud`, `cloud_service`,
  `cloud_label`, `is_hosting`) whose `cert_sans`/`cert_subject`/`hostnames`/`rdns`/`hosted_domain`
  names do not cover the domain; `internetdb_status: 'not seen by Shodan scanners'` adds a signal.
* Stale host (CR25): `eol_software`, expired `cert_not_after`, `tls_self_signed`, CVEs, FTP/Telnet,
  >= 3 `security_headers_missing`, `expires` within 30 days, `archive_last` older than 2 years.
* Ownership (cond 5): own = the engine's `own_asset` ownership or `OrgProfile.owns`; an address an
  own domain resolves to is own too unless the address is shared (noise/`shared_ip`/`is_cdn`), the
  domain is CNAME'd or its entry is `third_party_hosted`. In scope = entity tag `in_scope` or the
  run's ACTIVE scope (`options.scope`, a `core.scope.Scope` or its mapping: domains, CIDRs, URL
  prefixes; pivots never inherit it). Ownership is decided first: an own or in-scope host a feed
  lists as malicious keeps its severity ('possibly compromised'). Other third-party hosts get INFO
  and the `third_party` tag; other entities tagged malicious/phishing/c2 get INFO pivots tagged
  `infrastructure` and `third_party` (outside the org profile: never verified automatically).
* CR26 reads the core setting `attack_surface_min_assets` (default 20) through `rctx.setting`.

### E-mail spoofing inputs (P027 cond 3, 6)

`spf`, `spf_status` (`ok|none|permerror|temperror|could_not_verify`), `spf_lookups`,
`spf_void_lookups`, `dmarc`, `dmarc_policy` (the *applied* policy: the producer's discovery uses the
organisational domain and its `sp=` when the name has no `_dmarc` record), `dmarc_pct`, `dmarc_sp`,
`dmarc_rua_external` (`[{dest_domain, authorized: true|false|null}]`), `mx` (`['.']` = null MX) and
the `email_posture` table (`spf`/`dmarc` 'absent' = verified absence, 'could not verify' otherwise;
`mx` 'null MX (no mail)' / 'none (implicit MX)' / 'NXDOMAIN', or the dns_records attribute
`valid: false`: non-sending). Producers write these only from NOERROR/NXDOMAIN answers; a missing
fact is 'could not verify' and the rules stay silent. `spf_status` temperror (an unevaluated include
chain) or could_not_verify is 'could not verify' even when the top record is readable, unless the
lookup count is already above the limit (permerror). `dmarc_state(node, graph)`: a verified absence
at a subdomain inherits the organisational domain's record (its `dmarc_sp`, default `dmarc_policy`)
when that node is in the graph (cond 4 in the rule itself). An org-profile domain a feed tags
malicious/phishing is still judged (it is not an attacker's domain). The sending domain of the
phishing case is a domain reached by `sender_domain` from a seed (an analysed e-mail) or by
`email_domain` from an analyst-typed e-mail address. CR03's severity is LOW; terminal renderers show
rule `dmarc_reports_external_unauthorized` as INFO: they read `email_spoofing.TERMINAL_SEVERITY`
(open item for the WP14 terminal renderer, which does not read it yet).
