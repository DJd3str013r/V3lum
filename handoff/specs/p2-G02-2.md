## P045 `dnsbl` - DNSBL / RBL lookups via DoH (SpamCop, Spamhaus ZEN/DBL via DQS, DroneBL, UCEPROTECT, Barracuda, SURBL, URIBL, Project Honey Pot)

- council: **approved**, priority P2, default_enabled=True, provider_tier=community, terms=noncommercial, opsec=passive, shares_input=True
- category: ip_network; group: G02; inputs: ipv4, domain; produces: 
- auth: mixed; key env vars: V3LUM_SPAMHAUS_DQS_KEY, V3LUM_HTTPBL_KEY; hosts: cloudflare-dns.com

### Final conditions (binding spec - implement every item)

1. Per-list terms registry; a list is wired only once its entry is recorded. SpamCop and DroneBL are 'open' within their free-use terms. Spamhaus ZEN/DBL only through a DQS key on the plan legal decides (key-terms; commercial DQS for business use; key_plans.spamhaus_dqs); public Spamhaus mirrors are noncommercial and never used. Project Honey Pot http:BL is key-terms (company account).
2. URIBL, SURBL, UCEPROTECT and Barracuda are noncommercial/fair-use lists: off under the corporate profile until licence-cleared (their free-use terms recorded and allowed by legal).
3. Keyed zones (Spamhaus DQS, Project Honey Pot http:BL) embed the key in the query name: never through public DoH. They use only the system resolver through dnspython (optional extra; otherwise UNAVAILABLE reason_code 'package'), with the qname redacted in logs, cache and evidence (AR01).
4. Lists that refuse public resolvers (Spamhaus without DQS, URIBL, SURBL, unregistered Barracuda) are not queried through DoH and appear as ONE coverage line ('needs DQS key/registration'). Unkeyed lists that answer public resolvers (SpamCop, DroneBL) go through the core DoH layer.
5. Bundled per-list return-code tables with tests. Refusal answers (Spamhaus 127.255.255.252/254/255, URIBL/SURBL 'query refused', NXDOMAIN via public resolvers for Spamhaus) give BLOCKED ('could not verify', P321), never 'listed' or 'not listed'.
6. A per-run 127.0.0.2 test-point check per list: if it fails, that list is could-not-verify for the run, never 'not listed'. IPv6 nibble format tested.
7. UCEPROTECT Level 1 only counts. Level 2/3 (whole allocations/ASNs) and similar network-wide listings are never evidence against a single IP: at most info 'neighbourhood listing', zero risk.
8. One aggregated finding: 'listed on k of n queried lists (names, return codes)'. A single SpamCop/DroneBL listing = LOW. Listings tag spam, not malicious, and all DNSBLs count as ONE 'spam' family vote in P171 consensus.
9. Domain zones (DBL/SURBL/URIBL) for domains, IP zones for IPs. Lookups are capped per run (<= 10 lists per entity). http:BL scores are log-scaled; NXDOMAIN there means not listed.
10. Tests with the recorded DoH fixtures (SpamCop test point, Spamhaus via public resolver, Barracuda test) plus refused-answer and Level 2/3 cases.

Chair rationale: Approved 6-0 at median P2. legal_dpo's floor: per-list terms (Spamhaus public mirrors non-commercial and refused via public resolvers, DQS on the plan legal decides, the others only within recorded terms) and the http:BL key never through a third-party resolver. The architect's floor extends that to every key-in-qname zone: system resolver only, qname redacted, bundled return-code tables. cti_lead and analyst_ux: refusal codes, UCEPROTECT L1 only, one spam family vote, one aggregated finding. Terms is noncommercial for the NC lists, which stay off under corporate until licence-cleared. Tier community is the lowest list operator.

Synthesizer conditions (background; the final conditions win on conflict): Query through DoH; Spamhaus answers NXDOMAIN or 127.255.255.x to public resolvers -> 'query refused' (could not verify), never 'clean'; use Spamhaus only with a free DQS key (V3LUM_SPAMHAUS_DQS_KEY). Project Honey Pot http:BL needs an access key (V3LUM_HTTPBL_KEY). Domain zones (DBL/SURBL/URIBL) for domains. Each list's usage policy is low-volume/non-commercial: cap lookups per run; lookups disclose the IP to list operators (TLP gate). Gap fill: Spamhaus public mirror is 'free of charge for non-commercial use by small and medium sized organisations' -> corporate profile needs a DQS account (commercial DQS for business use); other DNSBLs follow their own fair-use terms (per-source terms registry).
Notes: Spam/abuse listing context for IPs and domains.

### Final conditions (binding spec - implement every item)

1. Every provider (VPNDetection, IPQuery, IPLocate, InternetData, ipapi.is, proxycheck.io) is terms 'unknown' and tier unknown until legal reviews it: disabled in every profile until legal records its terms (A1); then default_enabled=False, excluded at AMBER and above, --allow-unknown at CLEAR/GREEN until promoted (proxycheck.io and ipapi.is may be promoted with keys and terms).
2. IPLogs is excluded: it advertises active probing of queried IPs (response field 'probed') and documents no lookup mode that never triggers it.
3. Runs only on analyst-typed seed IPs (run_on='seeds'): never on pivots, never on IPs extracted from text, log or e-mail inputs, never for bogon, CDN or cloud-range IPs (P034). IPs of customers or employees (personal data) are enriched only with the offline datasets (P047, P035, P040, P034).
4. Offline lists answer first; online votes are asked only when they are silent. At most 4 providers are wired, each with its own fixture, status and min_interval (1 req/s); cache 24 h; optional keys (iplocate, ipapiis, proxycheck) in config.KNOWN_KEYS.
5. One shared vote helper with ip_geolocation (P051). Providers are asked one at a time and asking stops once 3 agree or 3 agreeing answers become impossible. VPNDetection and InternetData share a backend and count once.
6. A verdict needs >= 3 answered, agreeing, independent providers. Fewer answers give the attribute anonymizer_votes, status ok, and the wording 'inconclusive (insufficient votes)', never 'not a VPN' or 'clean'. Failed providers are listed as could not verify.
7. Output is ONE line with the votes, e.g. 'VPN: 3 of 4 providers (VPNDetection, IPQuery, IPLocate); hosting: 4/4'. Verdicts are context tags (vpn, proxy, hosting) feeding CR28, never malicious.
8. Use the P331 declarative collector for these adapters where it fits.

Chair rationale: Approved 6-0 at median P2 (cti_lead and legal_dpo voted P3). legal_dpo's floor decides the shape: all providers are unknown terms and tier until reviewed, IPLogs is out, and IPs that are personal data never reach these operators. That resolves the conflict between osint_investigator (IPLogs only for hostile IPs) and redteam_lead (never for hostile IPs). It also overrides analyst_ux's wish to run on IPs from pasted logs. The architect's floor: a shared vote helper, at most 4 providers with fixtures, the >= 3 vote rule. cti_lead's 'at most 3 per lookup' is met in the common case by asking providers one at a time. Unknown tier and terms: default_enabled false (A3 BR2).

Synthesizer conditions (background; the final conditions win on conflict): Vote across providers (>=3 agreeing votes for is_vpn/is_proxy/is_hosting verdicts); VPNDetection and InternetData share a response schema (likely the same operator) -> count once; ipapi.is detection flags and proxycheck.io need optional keys for full data; filter bogons first; 1 req/s per provider; cache 24 h. Implement via the declarative JSON collector (G15) where possible.
Notes: Anonymizer verdicts for IOC IPs (CR28) without paid services.
