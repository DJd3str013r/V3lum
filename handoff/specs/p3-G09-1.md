## P209 `ens_resolver` - ENS resolution (ensdata.net)

- council: **approved**, priority P3, default_enabled=True, provider_tier=community, terms=open, opsec=passive, shares_input=True
- category: crypto; group: G09; inputs: domain, keyword, eth_address; produces: eth_address, url, username
- auth: none; key env vars: -; hosts: api.ensdata.net

### Final conditions (binding spec - implement every item)

1. Module crypto/ens_resolver: api.ensdata.net/{name}.eth or /{address}; 404 'no reverse record' = EMPTY (no name), never an absent wallet.
2. Text records (twitter, github, url, email) are self-asserted by the ENS owner: POSSIBLE, labelled 'self-asserted by the ENS owner'; PROBABLE only when the linked profile points back to the ENS name or wallet (found by another module in the case).
3. URL, domain and e-mail pivots from text records are attacker-controllable: passive modules only, never LIGHT/ACTIVE contact, rendered defanged, expand=False for personal identifiers. Registry provider ensdata: tier community, terms open. Fixtures: name, address and 404.

Chair rationale: Approved 6-0 at median P3. cti_lead, legal_dpo and the architect accepted PROBABLE as drafted, while osint_investigator and analyst_ux showed that anyone can write any handle into a text record; osint_investigator's rule (POSSIBLE unless the profile links back) reconciles both. redteam_lead's passive-only rule is kept.

Synthesizer conditions (background; the final conditions win on conflict): api.ensdata.net/{name}.eth or /{address} (404 'no reverse record' fixture); text records (twitter, github, url, email) -> pivots as PROBABLE links.
Notes: Links ENS names, wallets and social handles.

## P213 `crypto_tx_graph` - Transaction-graph expansion with stop-nodes (Orbit-style BFS)

- council: **approved**, priority P3, default_enabled=False, provider_tier=n/a, terms=n/a, opsec=passive, shares_input=True
- category: crypto; group: G09; inputs: btc_address, eth_address, other_crypto_address; produces: btc_address, eth_address, other_crypto_address
- auth: none; key env vars: -; hosts: -

### Final conditions (binding spec - implement every item)

1. Not a module with its own BFS: an engine policy on transacted_with pivots (core expansion rules, AR07) with depth 1 by default, 2 only with an explicit flag (--crypto-depth 2), top-N counterparties ranked by value/frequency by the explorer modules and a per-case crypto request budget (setting crypto_case_budget). No new hosts. Opt-in: setting crypto_expand=false by default.
2. Stop-nodes are never expanded: labelled exchanges, mixers, bridges and pools (crypto_address_labels P358, walletexplorer P208, Blockscout tags), sanctioned addresses (P205) and high-degree addresses (> 1,000 counterparties).
3. Direction: expansion follows outgoing flows (cash-out toward exchanges). Addresses that only sent funds into an investigated address are labelled 'possible victim', counted, never expanded and not listed by default.
4. Counterparties outside the analyst's selection stay expand=False, labelled 'counterparty (role unknown)', and are never scored malicious by association alone (P302 caps transferred risk).
5. Output: a 'where the money went' summary (labelled endpoints reached, hop count, amounts, time window) that highlights the first labelled exchange (the legal-request target), plus a JSON/GraphML export; never a list of hundreds of addresses. Orbit (GPL-3.0) is used for ideas only (A6).

Chair rationale: Approved 6-0 at median P3. The architect's floor makes it an engine policy rather than a second BFS that would bypass the guard rails. osint_investigator's victim-protection rule (incoming senders are possible victims, never expanded), cti_lead's and redteam_lead's stop-nodes, legal_dpo's no guilt by association and analyst_ux's summary output are consolidated. An engine policy, not a data source: tier and terms 'n/a'.

Synthesizer conditions (background; the final conditions win on conflict): Uses the explorer modules (no new hosts); depth 1 by default (2 max with explicit flag), top-N counterparties by value/frequency, stop at labelled services (exchanges, mixers, sanctioned), budget per case; Orbit (GPL) ideas only.
Notes: Follows the money without exploding the graph.

## P214 `ton_explorer` - TON (Toncenter + Telegram +888 / collectible username owner lookup)

- council: **approved**, priority P3, default_enabled=False, provider_tier=community, terms=open, opsec=passive, shares_input=True
- category: crypto; group: G09; inputs: other_crypto_address, phone, username; produces: other_crypto_address
- auth: none; key env vars: V3LUM_TONCENTER_KEY; hosts: toncenter.com, tonapi.io, api.getgems.io

### Final conditions (binding spec - implement every item)

1. Module crypto/ton_explorer: Toncenter v3 at 1 rps keyless (key 'toncenter' as X-API-Key header raises it to 10 rps); user-friendly TON address decoding (base64url, 36 bytes, CRC16-XMODEM) in core validators with tests. default_enabled False (opt-in).
2. phone input applies() only to +888 anonymous numbers and username input only to collectible Telegram usernames, already in the case as fraud/threat subjects with the purpose recorded; never ordinary phone numbers, never enumeration of number or username ranges, never auto-expanded.
3. +888 number or collectible username -> NFT -> current owner wallet (a_TON_of_privacy flow, MIT attribution), reported as 'current NFT owner wallet of the collectible number/username (ownership can change; transfer date shown)': a PROBABLE lead with expand=False, never identity proof.
4. On-chain resolution through Toncenter only; tonapi.io and getgems.io receive identifiers only after legal records their terms and tier (A8). Fixtures for the address and NFT endpoints are captured before merge (only the chain endpoint was exercised). Registry provider toncenter: tier community, terms open.

Chair rationale: Approved 6-0 at median P3. legal_dpo's floor restricts this de-anonymising lookup to +888 numbers and collectible usernames of case subjects with a purpose and holds tonapi/getgems until reviewed; cti_lead, osint_investigator, redteam_lead and analyst_ux keep the owner wallet a dated lead; the architect requires the validator and fixtures.

Synthesizer conditions (background; the final conditions win on conflict): Toncenter v3 at 1 rps keyless; user-friendly address decoding (base64url 36 bytes with CRC16-XMODEM); +888 anonymous numbers and collectible Telegram usernames -> NFT -> owner wallet (a_TON_of_privacy flow, MIT) only for numbers/usernames already in the case; only the chain endpoint was exercised (verify address endpoints in tests).
